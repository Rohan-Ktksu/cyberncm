# Gate G1: is the mesh worth building?

Plan step 40 (PLAN.md section 6). This page sets the evidence against the five gate questions and gives **a recommended verdict for the team to decide**; the gate itself is not mine to close. Evidence is in `research/`; every sentence has a row in `research/CLAIMS.md`. Nothing is confirmed by an independent rerun yet (plan step 27). R8 was skipped by decision (it only matters if the mesh is built).

## The five questions

| Question | Evidence | Answer | If no (plan) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is there an alert policy a real network could tolerate? | R4 (`research/R4/README.md`), R12 block rule | **No.** No policy meets 80% of attackers caught in 5 minutes with fewer than one false alert per host-day on Unraveled in any of 5 seeds; only CSE-CIC-IDS2018 meets it, flattered by sparse hosts. | The product alerts and never blocks on its own |
| Does sharing help more than it harms? | R12 (`research/R12/README.md`), R7d to R7f not run | **No, on this evidence.** In simulation with 2 or 4 sites sharing speeds the first block (blocked at or before the first window: 0.44 to 0.71, 0.21 to 0.48, 0.28 to 0.45 at 4 sites under the two-site rule) but adds almost nothing to attack windows prevented (at most +0.011), wrongly blocks 44% and 36% more benign source-site-days on two datasets, and a lying site is not stopped when false alerts are common. A 30-second expiry removes most of the harm and most of the protection. | Single-site detector; drop the mesh, consensus and federation |
| How long must a site learn before enforcing? | R11 (`research/R11/README.md`) | **Not answered.** Refitting the site's own normal traffic is enough and needs no gradient training; 10 minutes of lab traffic was within 0.012 of full retraining, but that is the lab minimum and clean lab days, not a product setting. | The duration must be chosen on a network with day and night cycles |
| Which detector ships? | R6a to R6f, R6g (`research/V1/README.md`), R7 (`research/R7/README.md`), R7m, R3 | **v0 (= v1)**, per-site refit, alert-only. Stress tests: a poisoned learning period is a breach (6 of 20 families), mimicry and low-and-slow tests breach little but are weak; attackers with history are caught without leaning on source novelty; new benign sources are flagged heavily. | — |
| Shared model or per-site? | R8 skipped | **Per-site refit by default** (R11). Moot if the mesh is dropped. | — |

## Recommended verdict

**Do not build the mesh on this evidence; build the single-site detector, in alert and shadow mode.** Concretely for Block G:

- **Step 41 (single-site agent, API, database, console) is supported:** flow records, per-site refit on the site's own normal traffic, alerts and "what would be blocked", shadow mode by default. Its first job is to measure what the lab data cannot: normal traffic with daily and weekly cycles, the real false-alert rate and the real learning duration.
- **Step 42 (two agents exchanging directives, gossip, mesh view) is not supported as a feature.** A two-site replay already exists as an illustration (`demo/`); it must not be presented as evidence that sharing helps (CLAIMS N4).
- **Step 43 (Rust agent, consensus, attestation, federated learning) is not supported** and should wait until a much cleaner alert policy exists; its premise (sharing helps) failed here.

## What would change the verdict

1. **A far cleaner alert policy.** R12's harm comes from false alerts spreading; if a policy reaches near-zero false alerts per host-day with most attackers caught (R4's gap), sharing's speed gain would no longer be paid for in wrongly blocked hosts and the question should be rerun.
2. **Real sites unlike the lab split.** R12 splits hosts at random inside one dataset, with no latency or clock skew; real sites with different populations might gain more from sharing, or lose more.
3. **A poisoning defence that works.** The learning-period breach (R7c) would also let a hostile site shape what the mesh believes; learning only from an owner-confirmed clean period is the planned process, unmeasured.

## What this verdict does not rest on

- R7d and R7f (run on one site, `research/R7df`: induced and spoofed blocks breach on every seed, which supports alert-only), R14, R13, R5, R9 and R6e (reduced versions, none changes the verdict): none is needed for the "no" on sharing, because the harm shows without any attacker on the mesh. R7e's replay and malformed directives are untested because no directive protocol exists.
- Any independent confirmation: step 27 is pending; the kit is `research/RERUN/`.
